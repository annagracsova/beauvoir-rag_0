Dialogue with Beauvoir

A RAG-Powered Philosophical Interlocutor for the Works of Simone de Beauvoir

1. Project Overview

For my capstone project I have chosen a RAG-powered Streamlit application “In Dialogue with Beauvoir”. This project is largely a proof of concept, to see and evaluate, how literature reviews, philosophy or art history can be augmented with the help of machine learning und AI. 

This application allows users to pose philosophical questions to a simulation of Simone de Beauvoir, one of the twentieth century's most significant existentialist philosophers and feminist thinkers. Using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), the system bases every response in passages drawn directly from a curated corpus of de Beauvoir's actual writings, displaying the source excerpts alongside each generated response so that the AI's reasoning remains transparent and auditable.

For the corpus I chose two of her books “Second Sex” and “Ethics of ambiguity” as well as two of her interviews. This blend allows to have a peak in both her thoughts and works.

The application is built in Python using Streamlit for the interface, LangChain for the retrieval pipeline, ChromaDB as the local vector store, and the Claude API for language generation. It requires no cloud database, no user accounts, and no proprietary data: remaining ethical and avoiding using excessive resources is a priority of this project as well. 

The goal of the platform is to allow a direct communication with personalities from the past, to ask an AI to speak in her voice is therefore to stage a confrontation between her philosophy and the technology that attempts to ventriloquize it. That confrontation is the project's thesis.

2. Literature review

In The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947) and across her philosophical memoirs, de Beauvoir speaks of situated existence: we are always already embedded in a particular body, history, gender, and social position, and our knowledge is always shaped by that embeddedness. This makes her an interesting subject for AI simulation, a subject without these constrains. The research question, the project investigates, is therefore: Can a RAG system preserve the philosophical integrity and situated voice of a thinker whose entire philosophy insists that voice cannot be separated from situation?

The humanities have a long tradition of hermeneutics, the theory and practice of textual interpretation. RAG is, in a sense, a computational hermeneutic: it selects, contextualizes, and creates passages in response to a question. The project positions itself within the emerging field of digital hermeneutics, examining what is gained and what is lost when interpretation is partially automated. The project engages this dimension explicitly in its critical reflection, asking: what are the ethics of using AI to represent a thinker who wrote extensively about the violence of objectification and the importance of authentic self-expression?

3. Data Collection and Preparation

The corpus is assembled from legally and academically appropriate source categories. The Ethics of Ambiguity and Second Sex are de Beauvoir's most accessible philosophical works and as well as her documented interviews, with her most direct statement on existentialist ethics, freedom, and the ambiguity of the human condition.

All texts were converted to plain text, cleaned to remove formatting artefacts, and chunked into segments of approximately 400 tokens with 50-token overlap to preserve contextual continuity across chunk boundaries. Chunks were embedded using a sentence-transformer model and stored in ChromaDB. Each chunk will carry metadata recording its source title, enabling precise citation in the interface.

4. Model Development

Rather than relying solely on a language model's parametric knowledge (which may be incomplete, outdated, or hallucinated), RAG first retrieves relevant passages from a curated document corpus and then conditions the language model's response on those retrieved passages. This grounds the model's output in verifiable source material: a critical requirement for a project engaging with specific philosophical positions.

The RAG pipeline for this project operates as follows:

1.     The user submits a question via the Streamlit interface.

2.     The question is embedded as a vector and compared against the pre-embedded corpus chunks in ChromaDB.

3.     The 3 to 5 most semantically relevant passages are retrieved.

4.     These passages, together with the user's question and a carefully designed system prompt, are sent to Ollama 3.1.

5.     The model generates a response in de Beauvoir's philosophical voice, explicitly grounded in the retrieved passages.

6.     The interface displays both the response and the source passages side by side, with attribution.

Since the project uses a pre-trained language model rather than a fine-tuned one, the primary form of model development is prompt engineering: the design, testing, and iteration of the system prompt that instructs the model how to respond. This system prompt must accomplish several things simultaneously: it must instruct the model to adopt de Beauvoir's philosophical register and characteristic modes of argument; to ground responses in the retrieved passages rather than general knowledge; to acknowledge uncertainty where the corpus does not speak clearly; and to decline to answer questions that fall outside the corpus's scope rather than confabulate.

5. Evaluation and Iteration

The system was evaluated against both the questions related to the topics of corpus as well as broader ones. Main focus was on the core philosophical questions directly addressed in the corpus (e.g. on freedom, ambiguity, the Other). When asked about modern topics de Beauvoir did not address in her works, the model still remained in character and delivered adequate responses.  

The responses were evaluated on faithfulness to retrieved sources, philosophical coherence and register consistency.

 

After trying out several prompts I picked up one that while simple delivered adequate results, without making the model sound artificial.

 

The outputs demonstrated that the system is capable of providing adequate answers (s. Transcripts attached). When asked about the topics directly mentioned in the corpora, the model answers by citing this sources, rephrasing them and elaborating additionally, while still staying close to the original. Interestingly, when asked about topics seemingly irrelevant to the content of the books, the results were still coherent. The model even acknowledges the lack of knowledge in certain areas. When asked about climate change, the model makes an analogy to a concept mentioned in a book and follows it with a discussion about climate change and that it is not really her specialty.

After asking several questions, the user might notice, that the model starts to repeat itself. The reason for this lies in a very limited corpora. Adding more secondary sources, possibly diaries or letters would mitigate this issue.

6. Ethical Considerations

Simulating the voice of a historical figure, particularly one whose philosophy centers on authentic self-expression and the danger of objectification raises genuine ethical questions. The project addresses this through full transparency: the system always attributes its sources, explicitly frames itself as an interpretive tool rather than an authoritative voice and includes a persistent disclaimer acknowledging that responses are AI-generated interpretations grounded in source texts, not statements de Beauvoir herself made.

All texts used in the corpus were verified for appropriate use under academic fair use provisions or public domain status before inclusion. No commercially licensed translations are used without authorization.

 

7. Outcomes & Creative Output
The project output consists of a fully functional Streamlit RAG application deployable locally, with setup uploaded to GitHub for global access. 

 

The interface is designed with humanistic legibility as a core value: the retrieved source passages are always visible, always attributed, and always inviting the user to read further. 

 

This refers to the local model running independently on Ollama with llama 3.1, LangChain and Croma DB. One other aspect of iteration and evaluation, was deploying the model on the Streamline Online. Unfortunately, in the current setup it proofed impossible, since the deployment kept crashing and after several iterations of adjustments, it was decided to keep the system offline only. The possible solutions included adjusting the Python packages, changing the models from local to Grok API, using another DB, embedding vectors directly in GitHub. The last archived result was correct output from the system, but the sources were missing. The link for the app is: https://talk-to-simone.streamlit.app

 

Secondary outputs include transcripts of the dialogs mentioned above: the conversations between users and the system on a range of topics, submitted as AI-generated artefacts that demonstrate both the system's capabilities and its limitations (s. Transcripts attached).

 

8.Summary

All in all the project proved that RAG in digital literary analysis is a method that can be used in a variety of applications: from research to teaching. Chosen technological basis for the local setup is functional and can be extended for further research. With that, the research question has been successfully answered. A RAG system can indeed preserve the philosophical integrity and situated voice of a thinker to a certain extend.
